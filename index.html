How to use
  1. Allow microphone access, then pick Men or Women as your reference (default: Men).
  2. Produce one vowel continuously and steadily (e.g. /i/ as in heed, /ɑ/ as in hod, /u/ as in who'd).
  3. The figure follows your voice: the tongue, jaw and lips take the posture of the vowel you are producing, and the vocal folds vibrate while you are voiced. Pick a Target vowel to see its tongue as a dashed outline and match it (with the microphone off, picking a target previews that vowel).
  4. The Vowel space inset shows your production as a rose dot among the reference vowels; the faint trail shows your recent attempts (a dot every 0.1 s) — a tight cluster means you are consistent.
  5. Try “ah” → “mmm”: the soft palate (velum) lowers and the velopharyngeal port opens on the nasal sound, then closes again for the oral vowel. Watch the Nasality (velum) meter.
  6. With the camera on, try “thh” (as in thin, this): the tongue tip becomes visible between the teeth and a rose marker appears on the drawn tongue tip (Tongue tip readout: visible · dental). Honest limitation: a single front camera only sees the tip when it enters the lip aperture — for /l, ɹ, n/ the tip stays behind the teeth and is mostly occluded, so the acoustic tracker remains authoritative and the marker simply fades.
  7. Hit ▶ Play back to hear the last 3 seconds of your voice and compare it to the target sound.
